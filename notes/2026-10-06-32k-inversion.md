# A40, 32K context: the ranking of the rungs inverts

Job 22713173. `COMPLETED`, elapsed 01:16:07, exit 0, node `gpub019`. All nine
arms ran, zero failures. 25 prompts per arm from `longbench_v2_32K.jsonl`, 256
max new tokens, greedy, seed 1234, concurrency grid 1/2/4.

**This is the headline result of the project.**

## The inversion

At concurrency 1, the operating point at which essentially every speculative
decoding paper reports its numbers:

| bucket | DFlash k=7 | n-gram k=5 | winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2K | **1.86x** | 1.16x | DFlash, by 60% |
| 8K | **1.26x** | 1.14x | DFlash, by 11% |
| 32K | 0.83x | **1.08x** | **n-gram, by 30%** |

The trained head goes from clear winner to actively harmful. The retrieval rung
is the only one still paying at 32K.

The 8K note predicted exactly this, with DFlash near 0.95x and n-gram near
1.15x. Measured: 0.83x and 1.08x. Direction correct, DFlash worse than
extrapolated.

## Mechanism

| | 2K | 8K | 32K | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DFlash first-position acceptance | 0.601 | 0.451 | 0.296 | **-51%** |
| n-gram first-position acceptance | 0.601 | 0.563 | 0.552 | -8% |
| n-gram coverage | 0.132 | 0.161 | 0.212 | **+61%** |
| DFlash decay D (k=5, conc 1) | — | 0.219 | **0.402** | |
| n-gram decay D (k=5, conc 1) | — | 0.044 | **0.016** | |

Identical at 2K, unrecognisable at 32K. DFlash **predicts**, and prediction
degrades as the context to condition on grows. n-gram **retrieves**, and a
longer document contains more matchable spans, so it fires more often while
acceptance given a hit is essentially unchanged.

n-gram's decay is *lower* at 32K (0.016) than at 8K (0.044), which is within
noise of zero. Across a sixteen-fold increase in context its acceptance did not
move.

## Crossing points march left together

| rung | 2K | 8K | 32K |
|---|---|---|---|
| n-gram k=5 | 14.4 | 8.9 | **3.7** |
| DFlash k=7 | above 32 | about 12 | **below 1** |

The window in which speculation pays shrinks roughly four-fold per bucket step
for both rungs. At 32K, DFlash has no usable window at all and n-gram's is
three or four concurrent requests wide.

## Finding 9: the optimal draft length collapses with context

DFlash's acceptance by draft position at 32K, concurrency 1:

```
0.296, 0.087, 0.038, 0.017, 0.010, 0.007, 0.004
```

Dead after position two. It drafts seven tokens per pass and accepts 0.46 of
them, a 6.6% acceptance rate on drafted tokens against 22% at 2K.

Fit the marginal cost. With `R = 1 + k*c`, the measured `R = 1.766` at `k=7`
gives `c = 0.109` per draft position. Compare that against the marginal
acceptance at each position:

| position | marginal acceptance | marginal cost | worth drafting? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.296 | 0.109 | yes |
| 2 | 0.087 | 0.109 | **no** |
| 3+ | <0.04 | 0.109 | no |

**The optimal k at 32K is 1, not 7.** At k=1:

```
tau = 1.296    R ~= 1.109    speedup ~= 1.17x
```

Which would beat n-gram's 1.08x.

If this holds, the story changes in an important way. **DFlash is not failing
at long context; it is being run at a draft length appropriate for short
context.** The prescription becomes a choice of rung *and* k, with k shrinking
as context grows, and that is something an operator can act on rather than a
ranking they have to accept.

This is the same mechanism already visible at 2K, where k=15 lost to k=7 at
saturation: positions 8 through 15 contributed 0.156 tokens between them and
cost 0.67 in R. Context does to k what load does to k.

**One arm tests it**, at concurrency 1 on A40, and it is now the highest-value
experiment remaining. If k=1 lifts DFlash above 1.0x at 32K, the result is
stronger than the inversion alone, because it is prescriptive.

Confidence intervals must come from **repeated runs** instead. That is coarser

least twice, since the headline claim lives there.

## Concurrency honesty

Reported ceilings for full-length requests, per engine start:

```
off    c=1  5.12      off    c=2  4.75      off    c=4  5.12
ngram  c=1  5.12      ngram  c=2  4.74      ngram  c=4  5.11
dflash c=1  4.03      dflash c=2  3.72      dflash c=4  4.02
```

Every arm's ceiling met or exceeded its requested concurrency, so no row is
mislabelled. But the DFlash arm at concurrency 4 cleared it by 0.02, and the
DFlash arms consistently get 20 to 25% less KV cache than the others because
the draft head occupies GPU memory.

**This is a confound worth stating.** At 32K the drafter's memory footprint
measurably reduces the cache available for requests. At 2K it does not matter;
at 32K it is close to binding. A rung that needs weights resident competes with
the KV cache, and that cost does not appear anywhere in `tau / R`. It should be
named in the paper as a third axis of cost alongside acceptance and
verification.

## Caveats

- Single runs, no intervals, and the k=1 prediction rests on a linear R model
  fitted to one point.
- 25 prompts per arm at 32K, half the count used at 2K and 8K, so this cell is
  noisier than the others.
- Prefix caching is on throughout. At 32K prompts share less structure than at
  2K, so the hit rate differs by bucket and confounds absolute throughput
  across buckets, though not within-bucket speedup ratios.
- One drafter, one model family. Whether prediction-based heads generally decay
  faster than retrieval remains a conjecture, now supported by three points on
  one curve rather than two.
