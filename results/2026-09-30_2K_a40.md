## What this does and does not show

**Does not show** that speculation is useless. All 100 prompts were submitted at
once, so the engine ran them at whatever concurrency it chose, roughly 100 in
flight. At that load the GPU is compute-bound, a verification pass costs far
more than one plain decode step, and speculation loses despite good acceptance.
This is one uncontrolled high-load point, not the concurrency axis the proposal
specifies (1, 8, 32).

**Does show**, and these survive the caveat because acceptance is independent of
how the batch was scheduled:

1. **n-gram beats DFlash on acceptance at the same draft length.** 2.979 against
   2.545 at k=5. Its per-position curve decays far more slowly (0.608, 0.457,
   0.364, 0.297, 0.254 against 0.656, 0.401, 0.240, 0.148, 0.100). DFlash starts
   higher at position 1 and collapses after position 2. On LongBench-v2 QA, where
   answers quote the context, the free lookup is the better drafter. This is the
   "mechanisms of opposite type" premise of the proposal, visible in the first run.
2. **DFlash at k=15 wastes most of its drafts.** Acceptance is below 0.03 past
   position 9. k=7 gives higher throughput (348.5 against 316.3) with only
   slightly lower accepted length.
3. **Comparing rungs at their native k is misleading.** Raw accepted length ranks
   DFlash k=15 (2.823) below n-gram (2.979), but truncated to a common k=5 the
   gap widens the other way. Every cross-rung claim must use a common truncation.
